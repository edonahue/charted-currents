# Modern interaction references

These are **interaction and information-design precedents**, not templates to copy. Charted Currents should combine their strongest ideas with its own historical/cartographic visual language.

## American Panorama — Digital Scholarship Lab

https://dsl.richmond.edu/panorama/

Useful principle: **the map is the historical argument, not an illustration beside the argument**.

Borrow:

- large map-first experiences;
- direct exploration over wizard-like navigation;
- historical data that changes in response to map/time interaction;
- ability to move from broad patterns to local detail;
- editorial confidence: sophisticated scholarship can still be visually inviting.

Do not copy:

- any specific project palette, layout, controls, or visualization idiom;
- desktop-only assumptions where Charted Currents can remain elegant on mobile.

## David Rumsey Georeferencer / Compare

https://www.davidrumsey.com/view/georeferencer

Useful principle: **a historical map is a manipulable evidence object**.

Borrow later when real period layers arrive:

- overlay/opacity comparison;
- synchronized modern/historical geography;
- swipe or spy-glass comparison where it genuinely reveals change;
- explicit distinction between source image and modern spatial reference.

Do not copy:

- the utilitarian tool-heavy visual density;
- controls that belong to a georeferencing workstation rather than an exploratory public atlas.

## OpenSeadragon + IIIF

https://openseadragon.github.io/examples/tilesource-iiif/

Useful principle: **primary documents can remain high-resolution research objects inside a modern experience**.

Potential future role:

- deep zoom into maps, manuscript pages, printed records, or material-culture images exposed through IIIF;
- source viewer inside or adjacent to the evidence path;
- preserving institutional image delivery rather than copying giant scans into the app.

Do not add OpenSeadragon during Packet 1 merely because this future is attractive. The first source drawer can remain simpler until a real high-resolution use case exists.

## Maputnik / MapLibre style system

https://maputnik.github.io/
https://maplibre.org/maplibre-style-spec/

Useful principle: **cartography is a designed layer system, not a background-image choice**.

Packet 1 should treat basemap styling as first-class design work. OpenFreeMap's style is a starting dataset/style system; Charted Currents should deliberately decide which modern layers remain visible and how they recede behind project content.

## Historical reference board

`design/reference-board/`

This is the primary source for **historical visual vocabulary**. Study:

- fine line weights;
- hierarchy between land, water, route, label, inset, and annotation;
- measured serif typography;
- hatch/dash conventions;
- restrained hand color;
- margins, rules, and inset framing;
- how maps communicate density without modern card UI.

Do not copy a historical map's inaccuracies, imperial claims, decorative cartouche, or exact composition into the modern product without historical reason.

## Synthesis for Charted Currents

The desired combination is:

**American Panorama's map-first confidence**
+
**Rumsey's treatment of old maps as inspectable evidence**
+
**IIIF/OpenSeadragon's document fidelity when needed**
+
**MapLibre's modern cartographic/interactivity capabilities**
+
**the actual visual language visible in the project's primary-source reference board**.

The result should not look like any one of these products. It should look like a modern digital historical atlas that could only have been designed for Charted Currents.
