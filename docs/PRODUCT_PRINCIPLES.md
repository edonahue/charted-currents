# Product principles

1. **The data should make the historical world feel deeper every time the user clicks.**
2. **Connections are the product.** Separate sources become more valuable when they illuminate one another.
3. **Ordinary ships matter.** A traceable merchant vessel can be as compelling as a famous pirate.
4. **Pirates are prominent, not structurally privileged.**
5. **Provenance is always nearby.** Every meaningful historical assertion should be one click from evidence.
6. **Uncertainty is part of the product.** Use Documented / Probable Match / Reconstructed / Contextual.
7. **Coverage gaps remain visible.** Missing records are not evidence of missing activity.
8. **Context changes meaning.** War, disaster, weather, and law should affect interpretation, not sit in a generic sidebar.
9. **Primary documents are part of discovery.** Maps and records are sources, not anonymous textures.
10. **Serious content can still be delightful.** Use restrained motion and exploratory prompts; avoid gamified scoring and faux-pirate language.
11. **Build the small beautiful thing first.** Infrastructure scale does not substitute for a compelling experience.
12. **Aesthetics are acceptance criteria.** A generic developer map demo fails even if its data model is correct.
