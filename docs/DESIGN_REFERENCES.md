# Design and interaction references

## Sid Meier's Pirates! (2004/2005 era)

**Interaction inspiration only.** Relevant ideas: one navigable regional world, ports as meaningful nodes, ships coexisting in geographic space, wind/environment always present, time changing world state, and exploration through movement/adjacency.

Do not reproduce Firaxis assets, map art, icons, ship models, sounds, UI chrome, copy, or recognizable screen composition.

## Herman Moll West Indies map, ca. 1715

Library of Congress: https://www.loc.gov/item/gm71005442/

Especially relevant: trade winds, Spanish fleet/galleon tracks, political geography, inset ports, hand color, and maritime-chart language.

## Greater Caribbean Mapping, 1450–1850

https://greatercaribbeanmaps.org/

Use for cartographic discovery and as a model for treating maps as primary-source objects. Do not replicate its catalog mission.

## PARES / Archivo General de Indias

Use institutional citation/reference-code patterns to inspire the evidence UI.

## Rijksmuseum

https://data.rijksmuseum.nl/

Potential Dutch maritime art, ships, port scenes, portraits, and navigational objects. Verify object-level rights.

## Pirate Arcade — shadow sister

https://github.com/edonahue/pirate-arcade-web

Possible shared family DNA: deep ocean/navy, brass, sea/verdigris, restrained oxblood, warm paper, professional literal copy, subtle nautical ornament.

Differences: Charted Currents is light-first, materially more refined, archival/editorial rather than arcade, and not bound to Pirate Arcade typography/components.

## Digital-history precedents to study, not clone

Maritime Britain, SlaveVoyages, Atlantic Networks, No Dollar Too Dark, Prize Papers, and Greater Caribbean Mapping. Novelty should come from integrated persistent ships, contextual layers, source-aware uncertainty, and links between ordinary traffic and predation—not from claiming maps or network visualization are unprecedented.
