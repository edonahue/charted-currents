# Content and historical ethics

Charted Currents covers a colonial maritime system shaped by enslavement and forced migration, imperial warfare, dispossession, coerced labor, piracy/privateering, smuggling, and violent extraction. A beautiful site must not turn those realities into decorative atmosphere.

## Forced migration and slavery

Presentation should be integrated but visibly distinguished. Enslaved people must not be modeled/displayed as another interchangeable commodity such as sugar, wine, or salt. Preserve source terminology where needed for fidelity, but contextualize dehumanizing or ambiguous historical language.

## Piracy/privateering

The site may acknowledge the fascination of pirate history while distinguishing legal privateering, piracy, naval action, merchant activity, prize-taking, and contested legal status.

## Colonial place names

Use modern canonical names for navigation while preserving historical variants. Do not imply modern political boundaries existed unchanged.

## Indigenous history

Where sources permit, do not render the Caribbean as an empty European maritime stage. Include Indigenous places, names, knowledge, and communities when relevant and supportable.

## Archival silence

Do not fill missing records with confident narrative. Absence may reflect lost archives, colonial source bias, language/imperial imbalance, or access limitations.

## Visual guardrails

Avoid generic skull decoration around human suffering, “treasure” metaphors for forced-migration evidence, and gamified casualty/capture scoring. Delight comes from discovery and connection.
